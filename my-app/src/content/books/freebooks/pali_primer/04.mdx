#### 2. Declension of masculine nouns ending in _-a_ , (contd.)

<Divider />

_Ablative case_ - Case endings _-ā_ / _-mhā_ / _-smā_ are added to the nominal base to form the ablative singular. Case ending _-ehi_ is added to form the ablative plural; _-ebhi_ is an archaic ending that is also used.

<br />
<br />
##### Singular

1. nara + ā / mhā / smā = narā / naramhā / narasmā (From the man)
2. mātula + ā / mhā / smā= mātulā / mātulamhā / mātulasmā (From the uncle)
3. kassaka + ā / mhā / smā = kassakā / kassakamhā / kassakasmā (From the farmer)

<br />
<br />
##### Plural

1. nara + ehi = narehi (narebhi) (From men)
2. mātula + ehi = mātulehi (mātulebhi) (From uncles)
3. kassaka + ehi = kassakehi (kassakebhi) (From farmers)

<br />
<br />
#### 3. Examples in sentence formation
<Divider />

##### Singular

1. _Yācako naramhā bhattaṃ yācati._ The beggar asks for rice from the man.
2. _Putto mātulamhā pañhaṃ pucchati._ The son asks a question from the uncle.
3. _Kassako rukkhasmā patati._ The farmer falls from the tree.

<br />
<br />
##### Plural

1. _Yācakā narehi bhattaṃ yācanti._ Beggars ask for rice from men.
2. _Puttā mātulehi pañhe pucchanti._ Sons ask questions from uncles.
3. _Kassakā rukkhehi patanti._ Farmers fall from trees.
